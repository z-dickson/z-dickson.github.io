How this was measured

The source is a crawl of each nominee's campaign website: the homepage plus linked pages such as Issues, About and Endorsements. When a live site blocked the crawler, an Internet Archive copy from the same week was used. Sites with almost no text, and nominees with no site, are left out.

Before matching, lines about donations, privacy, cookies, text-message terms and online stores are removed, along with repeated lines on the same site.

  • Issues. Each topic is a list of keywords and phrases. For example, "Data centers" matches "data center" and "hyperscale", and "Artificial intelligence" matches "artificial intelligence" and a capitalized "AI".
  • Share of issue talk counts every keyword hit on a site, takes each topic's share of that site's total, and averages the shares within each party. Every candidate counts equally, however long their site is.
  • % who mention it is the share of a party's candidates with at least one hit.
  • Signature phrases were picked by hand from the phrases that differ most between the two parties' sites. Boilerplate such as store and privacy text was skipped.

The district map uses the Census Bureau's 2026 TIGER/Line boundaries for the 120th Congress, which include the districts redrawn before this election. They are clipped to the shoreline and simplified for the web, so lines are approximate up close.

Data center locations come from FracTracker Alliance's data center map (downloaded July 28, 2026). Each site is placed in a 2026 district by its coordinates. Sites with low location confidence are left out. "Operating" includes expansions, "proposed" includes pre-proposals, and cancelled or suspended projects are counted separately.

Caveats: keyword matching can't tell support from opposition. A Democrat attacking a Republican's abortion record counts as talking about abortion. Endorsement lists, news clips and press releases all count as site text. Incumbent, challenger and open-seat labels come from FEC filings.

Sources: campaign websites of 2026 U.S. House nominees, archived October 29, 2026. District boundaries from the U.S. Census Bureau (TIGER/Line 2026). Data center locations from FracTracker Alliance.
